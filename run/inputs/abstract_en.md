# Abstract

Two-column typesetting is the default for most engineering journals, yet the text layer of a PDF stores glyphs in the order the producer wrote them. We study whether layout-aware reading-order reconstruction improves downstream use of scientific PDFs. A three-page benchmark with a known ground truth is rendered from a controlled layout engine, so that block order, header and footer positions, table cells and equation numbers are known exactly. A naive extractor recovers 71.4% of body characters in the correct position, while a column-aware extractor reaches 98.1%. The gain is largest on pages whose columns are separated by a narrow gutter of 20 pt. We conclude that reading order should be verified block by block rather than asserted, and we release the benchmark and the verifier.
